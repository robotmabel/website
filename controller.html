Controller

A 6-DOF wish,
51 joints of safe motion.

One Cartesian target is wildly under-determined for a 51-joint robot. The controller turns that redundancy into a feature — a hierarchical, GPU-accelerated cascade that takes operator (or policy) intent, makes it compliant, makes it collision-free, and tracks it with gravity-compensated torque. All onboard, on Jetson Thor.

Front end · retargeting

Human in, targets out.

The operator never commands joints. Their pose is retargeted into robot-frame targets — a per-frame hand keypoint solve and a Cartesian body QP — and only then does the cascade resolve them into motion.

01
Hands · match keypoints, not angles

Human and ORCA kinematics differ, so equal joint angles ≠ the same grasp. We minimise the squared difference between human and robot landmark vectors over the robot's 16 finger DOF — the dex-retargeting formulation — with joint limits as hard bounds and a temporal term λ‖q−q₋₁‖² for smoothness. Warm-started, it finishes well inside one 60 Hz tick.

02
Body · redundancy is the point

Each wrist's 6-DoF pose becomes an arm end-effector target; the head pose drives the neck. Two 7-DOF arms, a torso, a lift, and a holonomic base are over-actuated for that — so a Cartesian QP picks the solution that stays off the limits, keeps the torso comfortable, and lets the base lazily follow to extend reach.

The cascade · interactive

Three loops, one motion.

Retargeted targets don't go straight to the motors. They descend through three nested loops — each a different rate, each a different job — bridged by zero-copy GPU memory so perception, motion generation, and compliance share one address space. Tap a loop to open its math.

Operator / policy intent enters at the top as a Cartesian velocity command vcmd; safe joint targets fall out the bottom. Force and a sensorless residual flow back up.

OUTERGPU admittance — compliance filterruns in the perception loop

The physical-interaction filter. It turns external force into compliant yielding, so the robot is soft to touch while it teleoperates — and the compliance offsets stay perfectly in sync with the intent stream for clean data collection.

virtual mass–spring–damper:  Md ẍc + Dd ẋc + Kd xc = Fext
reference twist (atomic buffer):  vref = vcmd + ẋc

Runs directly in shared GPU memory alongside the ZED Mini perception stack — vectorized tensor math (PyTorch / cuTENSOR), not a CPU QP. Because compliance and perception live in the same memory, the yield is synchronized to the teleop intent with no copy and no bus hop.

MIDDLEcuRobo — whole-body safety filter100–200 Hz · <20 ms

Instead of one single-seed differential-IK QP, cuRobo evaluates the whole kinematic chain at once — spawning thousands of trajectory seeds on the GPU and distributing motion optimally between the 3-wheel swerve base and the arms.

parallel trajectory optimization (MPPI / L-BFGS):
  minseeds cost( q(t) )  s.t.  ESDFnvblox(q) ≥ 0,  self-collision(q) ∈ ACM

Every candidate is checked against the nvblox ESDF (the ZED point cloud as a live GPU signed-distance field) and the structural collision meshes. The Allowed Collision Matrix masks hand-to-hand pairs — e.g. left index ↔ right palm — so bimanual cooperation never trips the limiter. Target rate 100–200 Hz keeps reaction under 20 ms as the map evolves.

INNERJoint control + gravity compensation1 kHz · RTOS thread

An isolated real-time thread pulls the safe joint targets from GPU memory and computes motor torque that tracks them while cancelling the links' own weight — and watches for unexpected contact without a single force sensor.

PD + feedforward gravity:  τcmd = Kp(qd−q) + Kd(q̇d−q̇) + G(q)
momentum observer (sensorless):  r ≈ τmeas − ( M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + G(q) )

G(q) comes from the Recursive Newton–Euler Algorithm at 1 kHz (Pinocchio, or MuJoCo's mj_inverse) on a CPU RTOS thread. Masking the residual r against the interaction volumes the ZED Mini expects lets the robot tell intentional manipulation from accidental collision — and drive Kd→0 on impact to yield safely. r is the Fext the outer loop admits.

Deploys on NVIDIA Jetson Thor (Blackwell) — perception, motion generation, and admittance all in one GPU address space; only the 1 kHz dynamics thread sits on an isolated CPU core.

Mode 1 · Gravity compensation

The arm floats.

A full rigid-body model computes the gravity torque G(q) for every link and feeds it forward, so each joint exactly holds its own weight. The arms become weightless and back-drivable — guide them with a fingertip and they stay where you leave them. This is the inner loop's feedforward term, and the backbone of kinesthetic teaching and safe shared workspaces.

Mode 2 · Whole-body compliance

Push the arm, move the robot.

The external wrench is estimated from joint torque by the momentum observer — no force sensor — and fed to the outer admittance loop. The base behaves as a virtual planar mass–spring–damper: push an arm past its comfortable workspace and the whole robot glides with you, then coasts to a stop. The exact same residual r runs on hardware off the motor torque feedback.

Under the hood

Redundancy, resolved.

Fifty-one joints, one objective. Here's how the extra freedom gets used.

01
Null-space posture

Base, lift, torso, and arm joints solve jointly. The spare freedom maximizes manipulability and stays clear of joint limits — MABEL rarely paints itself into a corner.

02
Mobile base in the loop

The holonomic base is part of the reach. Far target? cuRobo drives the body. Fine work? Hold still and let the wrist talk.

03
Bimanual coupling

The ACM masks hand-to-hand collision pairs so the two hands can cooperate — carry a tray, open a lid — without the safety filter fighting itself.

04
Torque at the actuator

Quasi-direct-drive motors close a high-rate current loop, so commanded torques become real ones — the foundation gravity comp and admittance stand on. See firmware for the bus and timing.

05
Perception in the safety loop

The ZED Mini point cloud becomes an nvblox ESDF the middle loop checks every candidate against — the obstacle map and the motion plan live in the same GPU memory. See software.

06
Shared with everything

The same cascade serves teleop and learned policies. They supply intent; the cascade makes it feasible, safe, and smooth.
