FAQ

What is MABEL?

MABEL is a mobile bimanual robot: a holonomic swerve base, a 0.635 m lift, a torso, two 7-DOF arms, a 3-DOF head and, as standard, two 17-DOF ORCA hands, for 56 functional degrees of freedom. The hardware and software are released under the MIT licence, and the robot you buy is the platform described in the paper.

What can MABEL do today?

It can be teleoperated from a Vision Pro, an iPhone, a browser or a gamepad; record demonstrations to LeRobot datasets; map and navigate a space with a lidar through SLAM and Nav2; and run trained policies on its on-board compute. A MuJoCo twin of the whole robot ships with it, so work can start before the crate arrives.

How big is MABEL?

The base is 0.49 m across. The lift travels 0.635 m, placing the shoulders anywhere from table height to above a standard counter, and the arms span 2.23 m fingertip to fingertip. Power comes from two on-board battery packs on 24 V and 12 V rails.

Is MABEL safe around people?

A hardware E-stop de-energises every actuator independently of the computer, every command path is covered by a watchdog, and a tip-over model bounds the base's motion by the robot's centre of mass. The arms are back-drivable with torque limits. MABEL is a research platform rather than a certified consumer product, and it should be operated under supervision as described in the safety chapter of the wiki.

What is in the box?

An assembled robot arrives calibrated and burn-in tested, with the compute and sensors you chose fitted, two battery packs and a charger, the hardware E-stop, a USB-CAN bench adapter and its calibration report. A kit contains every plate, extrusion and printed part, the actuators with their bus addresses set, the harness and boards, the compute and sensors, fasteners bagged by chapter, and a printed build guide. The full lists are shown above.

When will it ship?

A parts kit ships in about two weeks. An assembled robot is built, calibrated and burn-in tested before it ships, which takes about a month. Spare parts ship within a few days. Every order can be followed from your account.

How do I pay?

Checkout is handled by Stripe. You can pay in full, or reserve a robot with a 10% deposit and settle the balance by invoice before it ships. All prices are in US dollars.

How is it delivered, and who pays duties?

Robots ship crated by freight; the freight cost is quoted after checkout. Parts ship by tracked courier. Import duties and local taxes are the responsibility of the buyer.

What does the warranty cover?

Assembled robots and every part manufactured by us are covered for twelve months. Pass-through components such as actuators, cameras and computers carry their manufacturer's warranty. Damage from collisions is not covered, but every part needed for a repair is available on this page.

Is there a return policy?

Unopened parts can be returned within 30 days of delivery. A robot is built to your configuration, so its deposit is refundable until the build begins.

Can I upgrade MABEL later?

Yes. Every option in the configurator is also sold as a part, so compute, sensors and end effectors can be changed after purchase. The design is open, and nothing on the robot is sealed.

Do I need a Vision Pro?

No. The iPhone app, the browser control centre, a gamepad and the keyboard all drive the robot. A Vision Pro adds whole-body teleoperation, with the operator's arms, hands and head mapped to the robot's.

Can I build MABEL myself?

Yes. The plans, the bill of materials with every vendor named and the illustrated build guide are free, and any part you would rather not source is available here. A kit contains everything already cut, printed and flashed.

What support is included?

The wiki, the community page and the people who designed the robot. Because the design is open, there is no part of the hardware or software you cannot inspect or change.
