Three independent swerve modules give true holonomic motion — strafe through a doorway, rotate in place, hold a heading while moving. 2D LiDAR underneath powers SLAM.

Why it's new

Not another arm on a cart.

Five things set MABEL apart from the mobile manipulators before it.

01
A first of its kind

The first open-source design for low-cost bimanual mobile manipulation — two dexterous hands, two arms, and a holonomic base, fully open and under $10k.

Open-sourceBimanual + mobile< $10k
02
Custom PCBs, one fast network

Purpose-built boards for the mobile base and body put every actuator on CAN and Ethernet — a single low-latency local network instead of a tangle of USB.

Custom PCBCAN + EthernetLow latency
03
Built for learning — a small embodiment gap

Body height, arm length, eye spacing, neck range of motion, and lift travel are all matched to a person's — so teleoperation feels natural and the demonstrations transfer.

Human-scaledNatural teleopTransferable data
04
Degrees of freedom that matter

Active perception, a tilting torso, and a vertical lift add the extra DOF that make teleop feel like reaching, not driving — a more natural operator experience.

Active headTorso tiltVertical lift
05
Stands on open shoulders

Built on proven open hardware — the ORCA hand, OpenArm, and the YOR paper — extended and integrated into one complete mobile body.

ORCA handOpenArmYOR

Cost is a design parameter, not an excuse.

Every component was chosen with two questions in mind: does it work for the task, and could a grad student in Lagos, São Paulo, or Beijing buy it next week?

3D-printed structural parts. Off-the-shelf actuators where they're good enough. Custom electronics only where they must be. No proprietary couplings, no five-figure encoders, no "contact sales" components.

The result is a research-grade mobile bimanual robot that one person can afford and one weekend can assemble. That's the contract.

Build it

One weekend.
One robot.

Full assembly takes a single, focused weekend with two people. Print, screw, flash, ship.

Print the structural parts.

~36 print-hours · PETG or ABS

STLs ship pre-oriented for a Bambu X1C-class printer. Total filament mass ≈ 2.1 kg, validated at 0.2 mm layer height and 25% gyroid infill.

Wire the swerve base.

~3 hours · two people

The base assembles as three identical swerve modules around a single power-distribution PCB. Connectors are keyed; you cannot wire it wrong.

Assemble the arms & hands.

~5 hours

Each arm and ORCA hand follows the published Open-Arm and ORCA build guides, with a MABEL-specific torso bracket included in this repo.

Mount the head & torso.

~2 hours

The lift column bolts to the swerve base; the torso slides onto the lift and clips into place. The head mounts on the neck via four M3 screws.

Flash firmware. Pull the repo.

~1 hour

One docker compose up brings up the entire ROS 2 stack. Calibration is automated — MABEL stretches each arm through a sequence on first boot.

Connect & go.

~5 minutes

Connect Vision Pro, open the MABEL app, pinch the air. The robot moves. Welcome to research.
