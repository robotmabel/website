04 · Teleop

Wear the robot.

Two apps put you inside MABEL. The Apple Vision Pro app streams your head and both hands — 27 joints each — at 60 Hz and renders the robot's stereo cameras back into the headset. The iPhone app drives the base with on-screen joysticks. Same wire protocol; everything below is real.

visionOS · manipulation

Reach in. It follows.

Put the headset on and you see MABEL's stereo feed wrapped around you. Reach into the scene — your hands drive the arms, a pinch closes the grasp, your gaze aims the neck.

Vision Pro capture · head view + wrist cams + bimanual reach
01
Stereo passthrough

The head ZED's left/right streams map onto an inward-facing sphere in RealityKit — you look around the robot's world in true stereo, with wrist-cam thumbnails for close work.

02
Clutch retargeting

A UMI-style relative mapping: pinch to clutch in, move your hands, release to re-anchor — no jumps, robust to where you started. Thumb-to-index distance becomes the grasp.

03
Whole body, one operator

Hand targets feed the whole-body IK; a lazy swerve base and a look-at neck come along for free. One headset drives all 51 joints.

iOS · the pocket console

Your robot, in your pocket.

No headset needed. The iPhone app is the lightweight companion — drive, pose, plan, and run policies one-handed, with a live 3D twin and the same camera feeds. It auto-discovers the robot on the network and falls back to an on-device simulator, so it always has something to drive.

iPhone · driving the base, live
Drive · joystick navigation

Two thumbs, holonomic.

Over a live head-cam feed, a left stick translates (strafe + forward) and a right stick turns and lifts. The command runs through the same swerve IK and tip-over envelope as every other path, with a speed governor and an always-present E-STOP. Drone-simple, robot-safe.

Arm & whole body · the 3D twin

Pose it on a model.

A procedural 3D twin — built from the same mabel_joints.json as the web viewer and MuJoCo — mirrors the robot's joints live. Drag per-joint sliders for the lift, neck, and both 7-DOF arms, or switch to Whole Body and move an end-effector while the whole-body controller coordinates base, lift, torso, and arms.

Navigate · tap-to-go

Tap a spot. It plans the route.

On a stylized occupancy map from SLAM, tap anywhere to drop a goal. The app runs an A* global plan around the walls, then a pure-pursuit controller tracks the swerve trajectory — the robot marker drives the route live. Save places like Dock or Staging and send it with one tap.

Policies · run a skill

Dispatch autonomy.

The policy library lists learned and scripted skills — CVAE visuomotor policies, navigation routines, and scripted behaviors — each with its kind, control rate, and an offline-eval success rate. Tap ▶ to dispatch; the robot runs it and reports back. See Learning for how the policies are trained.

Connect · sim or real

It finds the robot for you.

Open the app and it auto-discovers MABEL on the local network (mabel.local:9090) and connects. No robot around? It drops into Demo — an on-device 30 Hz physics-lite simulator driving the same twin — so the whole interface is live with nothing plugged in. Flip between Demo (sim) and Robot anytime; the views never change, only the transport.

The loop

What goes over the wire.

The device and the robot are two ends of one low-latency loop. Poses go up; video and state come back. Same protocol whether the far end is the real robot or the MuJoCo twin.

Uplink · device → robot

Your intent, 60 times a second.

The app sends a TeleopFrame over a WebSocket (ws://<host>:9090): a 4×4 head pose, a full 27-joint skeleton for each hand in the ARKit frame, the active mode (Arms & Hands or Base Driving), and — in driving mode — the nav-joystick axes. A reset message re-homes the sim; ping/pong measures latency.

Head 4×427 joints/handMode + joysticksJSON envelope
Downlink · robot → device

Vision and state, straight back.

The bridge renders the robot's cameras off-screen and serves them as plain MJPEG over HTTP (:8080) — head-left and head-right for the two eyes, plus wrist thumbnails — pulled directly into the app's web view, no custom decoder. A parallel RobotState message returns battery, mode, joint positions, diagnostics, and round-trip latency.

Built for data

Every session is a dataset.

A

Capture by default

Head + wrist cameras, joint states, actions, and the goal frame stream to disk in lockstep — ALOHA/ACT-compatible HDF5.

B

Intent in, motion out

Your hand and gaze become a 6-DOF target; the whole-body controller works out how 51 joints honor it.

C

Sim or real, same app

Point the device at the MuJoCo bridge or the robot — identical wire protocol, so you debug retargeting safely first.
