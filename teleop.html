One bridge, three clients: this console, the iPhone app, and the Vision Pro app all speak the same teleop_frame / joint_command / control_mode / external_force protocol to server/sim_teleop_bridge.py, and mirror the same robot_state. Start it with server/start_teleop.sh — the page links up by itself, cycling three paths every 5 s, exactly like the apps: Wi-Fi (auto-discovered — leave the field blank and it races the bridge on this machine, the host it was served from, and your last-used LAN IP, the browser analog of the Vision Pro app's Bonjour scan) → VPN (its Tailscale IP / ts.net name) → Relay (the secure internet relay — a token-gated wss://<host>/teleop?key=… on a public VPS the robot dials out to, so you can drive from anywhere with no VPN app and nothing exposed inbound). Cameras follow the winning path — MJPEG on port 8080 over Wi-Fi, or the same token-gated HTTPS edge over the relay.

Native apps · one console

One console. Five tabs.

Open MABEL on an iPhone or an Apple Vision Pro and you land in the same place — five tabs along the bottom. Four are things you do; the fifth sets the rules. We named them for what they accomplish, not for the robotics underneath, so anyone can pick it up.

Pilot 01

Drive and look. Twin joysticks over the live camera — one hand glides the base, the other turns and lifts. Every run records itself.

Manipulate 02

Pose the body. Grab the on-screen twin and the arms follow — you choose how firmly it holds and how much of the body comes along.

Navigate 03

Send it somewhere. Tap a spot on the map; it plans a path around obstacles, drives itself there, and parks.

Autonomy 04

Hand it off. Ask in plain words, run a learned skill, or schedule a chore — then just watch it work.

Settings 05

Set the rules. Who may drive, how fast it's ever allowed to go, and exactly what it should warn you about.

From first principles

Three plain choices.

Strip away the jargon and commanding a 51-joint robot is three questions a child could answer: what moves, how you tell it, and how it should feel. Pick one from each — that's the whole interface, the same on the phone and in the headset.

Safety

Safe by construction, not by manual.

Safety isn't a settings page you're trusted to read — it's the spine of the interface. Three promises hold on every screen, on both devices, whether you're an operator or a first-time guest.

A

Stop, instantly

A red STOP rides every screen and never scrolls away — one tap freezes all motion. Sleep the phone, lift the headset, or lose the link and the robot freezes itself: a heartbeat means it only moves while a human is actively holding on.

B

Never too fast, never past a limit

A speed dial scales everything from a crawl to full tilt. Hard speed caps live on the robot itself, every joint stops short of its mechanical limit, and letting go holds the pose under gravity comp — nothing springs back at you.

C

Only the right hands, when armed

One driver holds the controls at a time; the robot must be explicitly armed to move; a stored access code gates every connection; and a live health panel flags a hot motor or a joint near its limit before it bites.

Additional · Mac-side setup

Run the bridge, reach it anywhere.

All three clients connect to one process — server/sim_teleop_bridge.py on a Mac — which drives the MuJoCo twin or the real robot and serves the cameras. You only need this to go past the in-browser sim. One command, three networks.

01
One command

server/start_teleop.sh brings up physics + cameras. Intent arrives on ws://<host>:9090 as a teleop_frame; the cameras leave as plain MJPEG over HTTP on :8080, kept off the control socket.

02
Three ways in

Wi-Fi (Bonjour auto-discovery, lowest latency) → VPN (the Mac's Tailscale IP / ts.net name, any network) → Relay (token-gated public VPS). Each client just tries them in order — no mode switch.

03
Secure by construction

For the public path the robot dials out to the relay; Caddy enforces TLS + a ?key= token (403 without it). Nothing is exposed inbound. Full architecture, security model, and the live monitor → Software · Server & bridge.
