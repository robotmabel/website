Everything passes one gate.

Retargeting produces arm and hand references. A joint-velocity projection enforces joint bounds and capsule-clearance constraints, while gravity- and friction-compensated impedance controls the arms and torso.

Paper · collision avoidance and compliant control
q̇* = arg minq̇ ½ ‖q̇ − q̇des‖² q̇des = (qIK − q) / ΔtIK reference → desired velocity q̇min ≤ q̇ ≤ q̇maxjoint and velocity limits −Jc q̇ ≤ κ (dc − ds), ∀ c ∈ Ccapsule-clearance constraint τ = τg(q) + τf(q̇) + K(q* − q) + D(q̇* − q̇)

The paper projects the two arm-and-wrist IK solutions (16 coordinates) onto the nearest admissible joint velocity. Jc = ∂dc/∂q is the clearance Jacobian for capsule pair c, ds is the margin, and κ sets the approach-rate bound. The arms and torso then track references with gravity and friction compensation plus low-gain impedance; hands, neck and lift use separate position control.

The motion model

It knows when it would tip.

A model-based envelope limits base acceleration as the lift raises the center of mass. The browser illustration below shows how the support polygon, steering and lift height affect that envelope.

Drive it: raise the lift, slam the brakes, then switch the model off and try again. The widget integrates the constants in controller/mabel/motion_model/tip_over.py — nothing here is animated by hand.

Paper · three-module swerve and height-aware limits
vj = v + ω S rj,   S = [0 −1; 1 0],   j ∈ {1,2,3} ac ≈ v̇ + ω × v + ω̇ × rc − ω²rc hc(zlift) = h0 + kh zliftCoM height fit from the model akmax = g ℓk / [σk hc(zlift)]edge-wise acceleration bound u = maxk (−nkT ac / akmax) ≤ 1scale the base command to stay within the envelope

The base-frame twist is νb = (v, ω). Module position rj is measured from the center of rotation; its velocity direction sets steering and its magnitude sets drive speed, scaled by cos ej for steering error. Each support edge has outward normal nk, CoM lever arm ℓk, and safety factor σk ≥ 1. The approximation fixes the planar CoM offset rc and neglects upper-body CoM motion.

Retargeting

Looking around isn't driving.

Head and wrist tracking estimate a body heading. A comfort box separates leaning from walking; shoulder-relative reach maps the wrists, and palm-frame fingertip targets drive the fingers.

Paper · operator state, body and neck
[Δr; Δz] = (wRb)T (wph − wph,a) Δrlean = clipB(Δr),   Δrwalk = Δr − Δrlean B = [−0.02, 0.18] × [−0.12, 0.12] mforward/lateral comfort box qtorso* = qtorso,0 − k Δxlean zlift* = zlift,a + Δz orbase* = orbase,a + Δrwalk qneck* = ypr((wRb)T wRh) − [0, qtorso*, 0]T

wRb = Rz(ψ) is the estimated gravity-aligned body-heading frame. The bounded displacement becomes torso lean; the residual becomes walking intent. Neck angles reproduce head orientation relative to that heading, with torso pitch removed. Subscript a marks a latched anchor; * marks a reference.

Arm and finger retargeting objectives
rpeᵢ* = ror,i + si (wRb)T (wpi − woh,i),   si = Lir/Lih qIK = arg minq ‖rpeᵢ(q) − rpeᵢ*‖² + wdir Σj∈U(1 − uj(q)Tuj*) + wrest‖q − qrest‖² subject to qmin ≤ q ≤ qmax,   q ∈ R⁸ per arm and wrist g* = arg ming ‖P(g) − P̂‖F² + wpinch Σm‖δm(g) − δ̂m‖² subject to gmin ≤ g ≤ gmax,   g ∈ R¹⁶ per hand

Arm IK matches palm position and palm-normal, finger-axis and elbow directions, using warm-started damped Gauss–Newton. Finger retargeting matches fingertip positions P and thumb-to-fingertip vectors δ in the palm frame. The wrist is already part of the eight-coordinate arm solve.

16× HLS3915 fingers + 1× HLS3930 wrist on a 1 MHz TTL chain; USB adapter auto-detected.

3-DOF neck (yaw / pitch / roll) via our dynamixel_neck library.

06

Lift · RP2040 Pico

MicroPython, BTS7960 + quad encoder, cascaded position + velocity, host lib + iOS GUI.

Make the numbers true.

Bring-up runs in a browser: a per-module status · control · calibration studio that walks the robot module by module — Arm, Base, Neck, Lift, Hand L, Hand R — and writes what it measures into one file the whole robot reads.

Every joint gets its own zero and its own stops

Each module opens on a Control tab (enable torque, jog the joint) and a Calibration tab. The lift is the clearest example: ⟳ Auto-calibrate — an armed two-click confirm, because the column moves — fires the Pico's own floor→ceiling stall-seek, and the studio records the measured travel window (min_pos / max_pos / span) under the lift/pico slice. The height slider only appears once that window exists, so you can't drive a column whose limits nobody has measured.

Files as truth, served over the wire

Nothing is hard-coded in a node. Calibration lands in a human-readable JSON file — diffable, reviewable, shareable — and a thin service answers Zenoh queries against it, so a node asks mabel/calib/arm/openarm-dual at startup and gets either one value or the whole slice. Three families share the tree: calibration (written here), network identity (human-set), and nominal physical (seeded from the MuJoCo model) — the as-deployed counterpart to the as-designed model.

Don't skip the CoM check after any mass change: wbc_stability.py --only 4. If E4 shows >1 cm bias, update the tip-over constants — that measurement is what keeps MABEL from tipping.
