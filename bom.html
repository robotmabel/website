The number

It depends what you
put in its head.

Onboard compute is 27% of the as-built cost and the least MABEL-specific part of it. So the total is a scenario, not a single figure. Pick one — the table below follows.

Which number should you quote? S2. A Jetson Orin Nano Super runs the ROS 2 stack, SLAM, navigation, the 50 Hz state stream and the video pipeline; policies train off-board. Thor is the right choice for onboard VLA-scale inference, and it is what the physical robot runs today — but it is a $12,874 − $9,624 decision made after the robot exists, not a part of it. Read what's missing before quoting any of these as a build budget.

Cost is a design parameter, not an excuse.

Every component was chosen with two questions in mind: does it work for the task, and could a grad student in Lagos, São Paulo, or Beijing buy it next week?

The ring is the as-built split (S1); the running total above follows the scenario you picked.

The parts that are genuinely MABEL-specific — brackets, plates, covers — are the cheapest lines in the whole build. That is not an accident; it is what restricting custom fabrication to flat patterns and printed parts buys you.

Why it's cheap

Five reasons, not one.

"Low cost" is usually a marketing claim. Here it is five specific engineering decisions, each with a price attached.

A

Commodity where it counts

Quasi-direct-drive actuators reached commodity pricing through the quadruped market. Fourteen arm actuators cost $2,181 — a comparable set of harmonic-drive joints with dual encoders is several times that.

B

Three modules, not four

A three-module swerve base gives the same three planar DOF and removes one module, one drive motor, one steering motor and two controllers: $557.50 saved, paid for with a smaller support polygon the tip-over model has to respect.

C

Dexterity by servo count

Thirty-four Feetech bus servos give 34 actuated hand DOF for $1,234 — about $36 per hand joint. Commercial dexterous hands at comparable DOF are a five-figure line on their own.

D

No machine shop

Every custom part is a bent laser-cut flat pattern, a printed cover, or a two-layer PCB. That caps the custom-fabrication content of the whole robot at a few hundred dollars — and makes it replicable by anyone with a printer.

E

Compute is a choice

Everything that moves the robot is gated behind one control library and one wire contract, and the heavy learning runs off-board. That is what makes the $3,250 difference between S1 and S2 available at all.

F

Honest accounting

We publish the gaps too. Section below: what the purchase record never covered, what it would cost, and where that leaves the sub-$10k claim.

Against the field

MABEL (S2) against surveyed mobile bimanual platforms. Peer prices are manufacturer-stated or retail-listed; commercial platforms are frequently inquiry-only, so this supports an order-of-magnitude argument rather than a precise ranking.

MABEL is cheaper than every commercial platform surveyed by a factor of 2–10, and is the least expensive open platform that has both multi-DOF hands and an articulated neck.

Verification

What the audit changed.

Four lines moved in value. The net is small — and that cancellation is a coincidence, not a reassurance.

LineSheetVerifiedΔ

Three part numbers didn't exist

The sheet listed DMJ4301, DMJ4343 and DMJ4341. None of those are real DAMIAO part numbers. The linked listings and the repo's own build table identify them as DM-J4310 (×6, arm wrists), DM-J4340 (×4, shoulder roll and elbow) and DM-J8009P (×4, shoulder pitch and yaw, 40 N·m). The counts were right — 14 actuators for two 7-DOF arms — only the labels were wrong. Ordering from the sheet as written would have produced three wrong parts.

One open question

The sheet lists a single ROBOTIS 2XL430-W250-T for the neck. The hardware layer disagrees with evidence: the Dynamixel driver records that MABEL's neck reports model 1090 — an XC330-T181 — and the config says there are two of them, pitch and roll. This BOM carries 2× XC330-T181-T. If a 2XL430 is in fact fitted, the driver's identification table is stale and should be fixed in code. Either way both sources can't be right.

Documentation drift found along the way. The published build guide listed an RPLIDAR A3 (it's an A2M12) and Arducam global-shutter wrist cameras (both wrists have carried RealSense D405 units since July 2026). This page previously carried LED matrix eyes and a 13-inch torso touchscreen; neither appears in the canonical MuJoCo model, so neither is in this BOM. The earlier published figure of $9,860 with a nine-category split corresponded to no purchase record and has been retired.

Uncosted

What the sheet doesn't cover.

A purchase record is what was ordered, not a complete parts explosion. Seven categories of real hardware are on the robot and costed nowhere. Estimated here, deliberately kept out of the totals above.

Uncosted itemQtyEstimate

This is where the sub-$10k claim gets tight.

The largest gap is the ORCA hand structure: the sheet buys 34 servos but no frame, tendon, bearings, tensioners or fingertip pads, and the hand is tendon-driven and printed. The distribution PCBs can't be verified from the repo at all — no gerbers have been released yet. Budget the S2 number plus this range plus shipping and duties, which for the Chinese-vendor block are not trivial.
