Hardware · Bill of materials

One core.
Five choices.

A fixed core of 46 parts — every actuator, every plate, all structure and power — costs $7,897 and never changes. Everything that moves the total is five components you pick yourself. Configure it below.

Three builds

Same robot.
Three price tags.

Every one of these is the same mechanism. The differences are a compute tier and a set of sensors — each upgradeable later without touching the structure. Pick one to load it into the configurator.

The Jetson price shock of 22 July 2026. NVIDIA raised the price of every Jetson module and developer kit by between 33% and 101%. The AGX Thor kit went from $3,499 to $5,499; the Orin Nano Super kit from $249 to $399. The bill as first costed was $12,969; the identical robot today is $14,968. Nothing about the machine changed — which is the clearest argument on this page for treating compute as a choice rather than a component.

Cost structure

Where the money actually goes.

The build regrouped by what each part does rather than where it bolts on. This chart follows the configurator above — change a choice and watch it move.

A

Actuation dominates, structurally

Every joint motor, hand servo, swerve module and the lift column is $6,239 — 65.6% of the Recommended build. Cost scales with degrees of freedom and no sourcing decision changes it. That puts a floor near $6,000 under any version of this robot.

B

Dexterity is not the expensive part

The 14 arm actuators average $150.79 each and the 34 hand servos $34.75. The hands carry most of MABEL's degrees of freedom for 12.4% of the Recommended build — worth saying plainly, because the assumption is usually the opposite.

C

Compute is the only order-of-magnitude line

One item spans $399 to $5,499 — at the top end more than every actuator in both arms and both hands combined. It is also the only category that can fall tenfold without touching the mechanism.

D

Perception scales, and scales far

$1,523.35 in Maximum, $362.97 in Recommended, $264.97 in Essential — on an unchanged robot. The $68 step from 720P to 1200P wrists is the highest-value spend in the whole build.

E

Everything else is noise

Batteries are $93.62. Power and wiring, data and connectivity, and control and miscellany together are a few per cent, and there is no saving to find there. The real power question is headroom, not cost: AGX Orin at 60 W would force a battery redesign.

F

Ten lines carry the build

The ten most expensive lines of the Maximum build carry $12,475 — 83.3% of it. The other 40 items total $2,494. Those ten are the only lines worth arguing about.

Build or buy

Buy the motors, not the product.

Actuators are the most expensive thing in this robot. Sourcing them at source rather than inside a finished product is the single decision that makes MABEL affordable.

OptionBoth sidesvs. MABEL

The arms are an OpenArm

Built from the same DAMIAO motors the official product uses — 8009P at the shoulder, 4340 through the upper arm and elbow, 4310 at the wrist. The licensed manufacturer sells the finished bimanual system for $6,000 plus $480 US air freight. Sourcing the identical motors and CNC set directly costs $2,622.24, 59.5% less. The premium buys factory assembly, a moulded harness, a flight case, a gripper, CE and UKCA paperwork and a warranty — not more capability. Against two AgileX PiPER arms, MABEL's pair costs 47.5% less, carries one more DOF per arm and lifts four times the load.

The hand row is not like-for-like

MABEL's $1,194.61 is actuators and driver boards. ORCA's CHF 2,000 is a complete material cost including printed structure, tendons, bearings, fasteners and tactile sensors. Adding MABEL's printed parts and hardware would plausibly land near $800–1,000 per hand — still under half the ORCA material cost and a fifth of the assembled price, but the defensible comparison is actuators to actuators. Quoting the raw ratio would not be honest, so we don't.

Against the field

MABEL (Recommended) against surveyed mobile bimanual platforms. Peer prices are manufacturer-stated or retail-listed and commercial platforms are frequently inquiry-only, so this supports an order-of-magnitude argument rather than a precise ranking.

Custom metal

Seven parts, one upload.

The only parts not bought off a shelf. Upload each DXF or STEP to an online sheet-metal service and select the operations below. Quotes are 5052 H32 aluminium at 2.0 mm.

RefPart & operationsQty×1×2×10×50×100×1000

The case for a group buy. It is also why the upper base plate costs nearly four times the lower one at similar size: four bends, dimpling, hardware insertion and tapping against a plain laser cut. For a lab building more than one robot this is the easiest saving in the whole bill.

Open items

What's unresolved, and what's missing.

A bill that hides its gaps is not a bill. Three kinds of item live here — unresolved conflicts between the parts list and the robot's own firmware, hardware that is on the robot but costed nowhere, and two arithmetic slips in the build guide that this page corrects. None of it is in the totals above.

KindItemEstimate

Where the gaps leave the total. Carrying the estimated range, the Recommended build becomes $10,082 – $10,687. Budget the Recommended number plus that range plus shipping and duties, which for the Chinese-vendor block are not trivial. And resolve the swerve line before you order — it is the one item on this page where a reader following the bill as written receives three modules that cannot move.
