Build · Wiring

Two rails.
One computer.

What connects to what, and in what order. Six pages: power, data, and the sequence to bring it all up without finding out the hard way which connector was loose.

24 V primary12 V secondary7 links to Thor15 devices6 pages
The rails

One supply.
Four branches.

A single 24 V supply feeds four branches, and one of those branches makes the 12 V rail. Read both diagrams left to right: the box on the left is the source, everything to the right of it is fed by it. Colour shows the rail, not the part.

Primary rail
24 V
Compute, both arms, neck yaw, lift. Straight from the supply — Thor, the lift controller and the torso bus each get their own pair.
Secondary rail
12 V
Base, hands, expansion boards, neck servos. All of it downstream of one buck converter — which makes that converter a single point of failure worth knowing about.
Links to Thor
7
Seven ports in use carrying fifteen devices. Every cable on the robot ends at the Jetson; the head and base cameras go in direct because they need the bandwidth.
24 V 12 V USB / data wired but unused in the standard build

Never share a run back to the supply

Thor, the lift controller and the torso bus each get their own pair. A few extra metres of cable removes a whole class of fault that is genuinely hard to find later — the kind where the Jetson browns out only when an arm accelerates.

The buck converter is a single point of failure

Everything on 12 V depends on it: the base, both hands, both expansion boards and the neck servo bus. If the hands, the base and the neck all go quiet at once, check the converter before you check anything else.

Bring-up

Power it up in this order.

Work outward from the supply. Check each level before you add the next.

Start with nothing connected.

24 V supply only

Leave Thor, the torso bus, the lift controller and the buck converter unplugged. Check the voltage at the terminals.

Add Thor and the lift controller.

the two dedicated 24 V lines

Let Thor boot all the way. If the supply sags now, it will sag worse later.

Add the buck converter — with nothing on it.

check before you load it

Confirm 12 V at its output before you plug anything into it.

Add the arms, one line at a time.

left arm first

J1 + J2, then J3 + J4, then J5 + J6 + J7. Each group should hold position before you add the next one. The neck yaw motor goes last.

Add the base.

six drivers, three modules

Keep the wheels off the ground until the control loop is working.

Add the USB hub.

slowest devices first

Start with the lift Pico and the audio device, then the cameras and the lidar.

What this guide does not cover. CAN and serial signal wiring — it shares the same looms but needs its own document. And fuse ratings and wire gauge: measure the stall current on your own build first rather than copying a number from here.
