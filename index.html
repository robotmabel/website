Abstract

A whole-body robot you can actually build.

A research-grade mobile manipulator, mostly 3D-printed. Two 7-DOF arms, two 17-DOF ORCA hands, a holonomic base, an active head — 51 joints in all. One Jetson Thor runs everything, untethered. Vision Pro teleop doubles as the data engine. All of it open.

Open hardware, sub-$10k

3D-printed, off-the-shelf, twin 17-DOF hands. Full BOM and CAD released.

Whole-body control

Base to fingertips, one system. The body reaches — not just the arm.

Vision Pro teleop

Hand and gaze at sub-100 ms. Every session trains the robot.

Long-horizon autonomy

Learned skills stitched to navigation. Multi-step, onboard.

System architecture

One system, five layers.

MABEL is vertically integrated. Operator intent and learned policies enter at the top and descend through onboard intelligence, a single ROS 2 graph, and per-MCU firmware until they become torque at 51 joints — while joint state, odometry, and vision flow back up. A MuJoCo digital twin mirrors every interface, so the same code runs in simulation and on hardware.

Figure 1. The MABEL stack. Commands flow down (intent → joint targets); joint state, odometry, and vision flow up. The digital twin shares the URDF and the ROS 2 interfaces, so behavior validated in sim deploys with a single flag.

01
One source of geometry

A single URDF generates the MJCF for simulation, the on-robot kinematics, and the web's articulated viewer — sim and hardware can't silently diverge.

02
One graph, two worlds

Every firmware bus surfaces in ROS 2 behind standard topics; the same graph drives the real robot or the twin — use_sim:=true is the only difference.

03
Base to fingertips, one solve

The whole-body controller resolves base, lift, torso, and arms against one task — a 6-DOF wish becomes coordinated motion across 51 joints.

04
Teleop is the data engine

Every teleoperated session is logged as a labeled trajectory that trains the policies — the same interfaces that drive the robot also feed the learning loop.
