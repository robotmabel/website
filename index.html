Abstract

A whole-body robot you can actually build.

Mobile bimanual robots are becoming the platform for learning everyday, long-horizon manipulation, but open ones give up mobility, working height, dexterous hands or active gaze. MABEL keeps all of them: a fully open-source, 56-DOF platform (holonomic base, 0.635 m lift, torso, active head, two 7-DOF arms, two 17-DOF hands) from $8,056 in parts, with no machine shop. One Apple Vision Pro drives the whole body with no hand-held controllers: morphology-aware retargeting maps your head, wrists and fingers onto the robot's body frame, with arm-collision filtering and model-based limits on the base. On hardware: 82 ms LAN glass-to-glass latency and 18.2 mm return consistency.

v1.0 · as built · no renders

Cheap, dexterous, whole-body

From $8,056 in parts ($9,283 recommended). Two 17-DOF hands, two 7-DOF arms, a 3-DOF active head, and a holonomic swerve base — a 0–2.23 m fingertip band on a 0.49 m footprint. The only open platform that is holonomic, hand-dexterous, and neck-articulated under $10k.

Modular by construction

Every subsystem is its own project on its own bus — swap an arm, a hand, the base, or the head without touching the rest. Extrusion + sheet metal; no machine shop; open CAD (CERN-OHL-S-2.0), firmware and software (Apache-2.0).

Simulation-complete

A MuJoCo twin runs the production control code unmodified at 500 Hz across 35 released environments. Same URDF, same ROS 2 interface — use_sim:=true is the only difference.

One integrated system

Teleop (Vision Pro, iPhone, browser), electronics and firmware, data collection, and training are one stack: every command passes one wire protocol and one whole-body QP — and every session is a training demonstration.

Real robot · no renders

Home, shop, street.

Housework in a real apartment, the bench it was built on, and nights on the waterfront. Wherever it moves, a pilot is flying it; each home clip is one unedited take sped up to fifteen seconds.

Under the hood

One platform, end to end.

Every one of those pilots — headset, phone, browser, webcam — speaks to the same stack. MABEL is vertically integrated: operator intent and learned policies enter at the top and descend through onboard intelligence, a single ROS 2 graph, and per-MCU firmware until they become torque at 56 joints — while joint state, odometry, and vision flow back up. A MuJoCo twin mirrors every interface. Hover any block for what it does; click to open its page.

One source of geometry

One MuJoCo model is the source: the twin, the ROS URDF, the app rigs and this page's viewer are all generated from it, so sim and hardware can’t silently diverge.

02
One graph, two worlds

Every firmware bus surfaces in ROS 2 behind standard topics; use_sim:=true is the only difference between the robot and the twin.

03
Base to fingertips, one solve

The whole-body controller resolves base, lift, torso and arms against one task — a 6-DOF wish becomes motion across 56 joints.
