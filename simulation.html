03 · Simulation

A twin that tells the truth.

A high-fidelity twin in MuJoCo and Isaac Lab, from the same URDF as the real robot. Train policies, tune control, validate every change — before it touches hardware.

Why a twin

Validate first, break nothing.

A 51-DOF robot is expensive to crash. The twin lets us iterate on control and policy at thousands of episodes per hour, then deploy only what already works.

MuJoCo · MABEL scene
MuJoCo

Contact-rich, fast, exact.

The MuJoCo model captures the tendon routing of the ORCA hands, the swerve-module kinematics, and the lift–torso chain. Analytical FK and damped-least-squares IK match the on-robot solvers exactly, so trajectories transfer without surprises. It is the default sandbox for manipulation and whole-body control.

Isaac Lab · parallel envs
Isaac Lab

Thousands of MABELs at once.

For large-scale policy pre-training and reinforcement learning, the same robot is instantiated in massively parallel GPU simulation. Thousands of environments step together, generating the experience that bootstraps a policy before a single real demonstration is collected.

Sim-to-real

One model, two worlds.

The gap is closed by construction: identical interfaces, randomized physics, and calibration that pulls sim toward the measured robot.

01
Single source of geometry

One URDF generates the MJCF, the Isaac asset, the MoveIt2 collision model, and the on-robot kinematics. DH parameters live in one config module — sim and hardware can never silently diverge.

02
Domain randomization

Mass, friction, actuator gains, latency, camera pose, and lighting are randomized per episode. Policies learn to be robust to the parameters we can't measure perfectly, so the residual sim-to-real gap stays small.

03
Identical ROS 2 interfaces

The twin publishes the same topics and message types as the hardware driver. A controller or policy node cannot tell whether it is talking to MuJoCo or to the real swerve base — so deployment is a config flag, not a rewrite.

04
Calibration in the loop

Measured joint trajectories from the real robot are replayed in sim; residuals tune the model's friction and gain parameters until predicted and measured motion agree. The twin gets more honest every time MABEL moves.

In the twin

What you can run.

A

Kinematics & IK

FK/IK sandboxes for the arms and whole body, with reachability and manipulability visualization.

B

Motion planning

RRT*, potential-field, and trajectory generation validated against the collision model before deployment.

C

Whole-body control

Tune the QP controller's task and posture weights against repeatable, ground-truth scenes.

D

Policy pre-training

Generate demonstrations and run RL at scale in Isaac Lab, then fine-tune on real data.

E

Teleop replay

Replay Vision Pro sessions in sim to debug retargeting and label trajectories offline.

F

Regression tests

Every code change reruns a scene suite; figures and metrics regenerate from scratch with fixed seeds.
