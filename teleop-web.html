Teleop · Browser

Teleop it. Right here.

The iPhone app's cockpit and manipulation console, rebuilt for the web — and wired to the same backend. It speaks the identical protocol to the same teleop bridge the iOS and Vision Pro apps use, auto-connecting the moment a bridge is reachable. No bridge? An in-browser sim keeps everything live.

The page auto-connects on load and keeps retrying quietly in the background — start the bridge (server/start_teleop.sh, the same one the apps use; see Server & Bridge) and it links up by itself. Once robot_state flows, the 3D model mirrors the server's MuJoCo sim exactly; press Disconnect to stay on the in-browser sim.

01 · Cockpit

Drive. Or reach.

The iOS Drive cockpit, joysticks and all. Drive: left stick translates the swerve base, right stick turns and lifts. Arms: each side gets a wrist joystick, up/down nudges, and a grip trigger — exactly the phone's layout.

Sticks are spring-loaded velocity commands with the app's caps (1.2 m/s · 1.8 rad/s); the camera chases the robot, and you can orbit it any time. In Arms, the joysticks nudge the arm joints at 1.4 rad/s and the triggers set grip — byte-for-byte the phone's teleop_frame + joint_command stream when a bridge is connected.

02 · Robot control

Hold it. Or push it.

The app's manipulation console, on its three orthogonal axes. Teleop — Guide a palm or set Joints. Feel — Hold (impedance spring) or Soft (it yields to your push). Reach — how much of the body joins in: arm, torso, or the whole base.

Drag a green palm ball — the arm tracks it like a spring. The realised pose streams to the bridge as the impedance target.

Sliders set joint targets; the robot slews to them rate-limited.

The robot only moves under external force — grab a ball and push. Your drag goes up as a world-frame wrench (external_force) and the back-drivable body yields: arms always, torso in Upper, the gliding base in Whole. Nothing springs back.

One canonical robot state drives both viewers — pose it here, scroll up, the cockpit shows the same robot. Connected, the axes go up as a structured control_mode spec (method · controlType · region · stiffness), Guide/Joints stream joint_command, and Soft pushes stream external_force — the exact messages WholeBodyView.swift sends.
